The bowshock Guide

Validation

What has been compared with data and with analytical results, and how far. The numbers below are copied from the repository’s READMEs (crates/bowshock-validate/README.md, crates/bowshock-cfd/README.md) as of this writing; they come from the validation programs in bowshock-validate and the tests of the workspace. Where bowshock and the data differ, the table shows the difference.

NASA TM X-2439 (Fox, 1971): sting-mounted nose-cylinder-boattail bodies, subsonic. From crates/bowshock-cfd/README.md, “Validation: NASA TM X-2439”, level 1, ogive (config 4):

The Euler base Cp is below the data in all six configurations (by 0.0116 to 0.0458). The viscous analysis (RANS) is closer (README.md, “Known limits”; cargo run --release --bin subsonic -- --quick --validate --rans): RANS base Cp +0.049 to +0.072 against measured +0.069 to +0.132 (Euler: -0.037 to +0.066). The Reynolds numbers per Mach number in this comparison were interpolated from the range quoted in the report and not verified.

The wall Cp over the forebody and cylinder agrees with the data to an RMS of 0.017 to 0.091 (ogive 0.017 / 0.023 / 0.060 at M 0.404 / 0.608 / 0.831).

With the RANS equations (cargo run --release --bin subsonic -- --quick --validate --rans, quick grids) the base Cp of the six configurations is +0.049 to +0.072 against measured +0.069 to +0.132 (Euler: -0.037 to +0.066); the Reynolds numbers per Mach number are interpolated from the range quoted in the report (crates/bowshock-validate/README.md, “Known limits”).

Stoney forebody

Stoney, NACA RM L53K17 (1954), Fig. 5: nose pressure drag of fineness-3 noses at Mach 0.8 to 1.25, free flight. The data were digitised from a poor scan (about +-0.01 in CD) and carry a tare uncertainty of about +-0.01; the Euler solver at level 1 reproduces the drag rise (crates/bowshock-cfd/README.md, “2. The CFD lies systematically 0.007 to 0.016 above the digitised data, at 1 to 1.5 sigma of the combined uncertainty. For the cone-cylinder (9.46 deg) the CD at Mach below 1.05 does not converge with the grid (sharp shoulder corner): M 1.0 gives 0.1215 / 0.1301 / 0.1351 at levels 0 / 1 / 2. That is the reason for Warning::SharpCornerTransonic. At supersonic Mach the cone nose converges to the Taylor-Maccoll value (M 1.2: -3.9 % / -2.0 % / -1.0 % at levels 0 / 1 / 2).

Arcas sounding rocket (D1)

Half-scale Arcas sounding rocket, configuration 1 (l/d = 18.20): NASA TN D-4013 (J. C. Ferris, 1967, Mach 0.60-1.20, Fig. 11) and TN D-4014 (C. D. Babb & D. E. Fuller, 1967, Mach 1.50-2.96, Figs. 4-5). Data are figure readings (+/-0.005 on the flat parts, +/-0.02 at Mach 0.90-1.00, +/-0.012 for D-4014). Compared quantity: axial force without the base (D-4013 C_A,corr; D-4014 C_A - C_A,c, the sign of the chamber correction inferred), on the body cross-section; bowshock: forebody_pressure + friction + fins. Motor-on base pressure, NASA TP-2005 (D2)

NASA TP-2005 (1982): strut-mounted ogive-cylinder, D = 6.35 cm, base at 82.55 cm (13 D, flat base), cold air jets through a 5.08 cm exit; free stream Re 6.56e6 / m, T0 325 K; jet total temperature 305 K. The nose length is not given (fineness 3 assumed). Data: measured base pressure pb / p_inf = (pt_j / p_inf) / (pt_j / pb) from Table II. bowshock: MotorSpec with the exit Mach and the exit pressure from pt_j / p_inf (isentropic), pb / p_inf = 1 + 0.7 M^2 cp_base. Gates: |delta| <= 0.15 and not Failed at every point; pb rises from Me 1.69 to Me 1.97.

Every gate fails. The measured base pressures above p_inf come from plume-induced separation of the afterbody boundary layer ahead of the strongly under-expanded plumes; the model’s base pressure stays near or below p_inf and falls with exit Mach where the data rise. Open issue: crates/bowshock-cfd/ISSUES.md row 13. The test is crates/bowshock-core/tests/validation.rs::tp2005_base_pressure_with_jet.

The Arcas table above was re-run with the same change: every value is unchanged to the printed digits (largest change 0.0001).
