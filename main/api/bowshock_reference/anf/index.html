Army-Navy Basic Finner (ANF) reference projectile: geometry and measured static stability derivatives, used to gate the 3D Euler stability solver and to judge the Barrowman-style (Extended) estimate.

§Source

Dupuis, A., “Aeroballistic range and wind tunnel tests of the basic finner reference projectile from subsonic to high supersonic velocities”, DRDC Valcartier TM 2002-136 (wind tunnel and free flight), with the supersonic free-flight part in Dupuis & Hathaway, DTIC ADA636861. Neither report was reachable when this module was written (DTIC was under maintenance), so the numbers below are digitised from Yayla (2021), METU thesis, Figure 3.10 (C_Nα vs Mach, p. 35 of the thesis, PDF p. 57) and Figure 4.4 (C_mα vs Mach, p. 40, PDF p. 62), which replot Dupuis’s wind-tunnel (WT) and free-flight (FF) points (https://open.metu.edu.tr/bitstream/handle/11511/93161/index.pdf). Replace them with the tabulated values from Dupuis when a copy is available.

§Digitisation

The thesis pages were rendered at 200 dpi, the axis frames located to the pixel, and the marker centres found by template correlation (green squares = WT, purple circles = FF). Marker overlaps were resolved by hand. Both figures are in 1/deg about the CG; they are converted to per radian here. Digitised, ±3 % on each of C_Nα and C_mα. The centre of pressure is derived, so its error is larger: x_cp − x_cg = −C_mα/C_Nα·d is a ratio of two ±3 % numbers, about ±0.1 cal at 3 cal of lever arm (more in the transonic scatter band, where individual shots differ by 0.05 in C_Nα).

Where several wind-tunnel markers sit within ΔM ≈ 0.04 of each other (Fig. 3.10 and 4.4 overlap them), they are averaged into one row; the free-flight shots are kept individually. One free-flight C_mα (M 1.376) was hidden behind a WT marker in Fig. 4.4 and is read by eye (±0.02).

§Geometry (Yayla Fig. 3.1, from Dupuis; one caliber = 0.03 m)

Verified against the figure: length 10 cal; cone half-angle 10° with nose length 2.84 cal (= 0.5/tan 10°) and nose-tip radius 0.004 cal; cylindrical afterbody 1 cal in diameter; four rectangular fins, chord 1 cal, exposed span 1 cal (3 cal tip to tip), root trailing edge flush with the base; fin thickness 0.08 cal; CG 5.5 cal from the nose; fins canted 2° (roll only, ignored by the pitch-plane methods). Yayla’s text says “20 degree nose cone”, which is the included angle (the figure dimensions 10.0° and 2.84).

Fin edges are not dimensioned in Fig. 3.1. Abiza et al. (XFlow LBM study of the ANF, p. 3, https://www.3ds.com/assets/invest/2024-02/prediction_of_finned_projectile_aerodynamics_using_a_lattice-boltzmann_method_cfd_solution.pdf) give a sharp leading edge and a blunt 0.08 cal trailing edge; Shelton, Martin & Silva (NASA, Kestrel/FUN3D, p. 2 and Table 1, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180006293/downloads/20180006293.pdf) a leading-edge fillet r/d = 0.005 and a 0.1 d trailing edge. The nose geometry above agrees with both.

§Measurement spread

Two wind tunnels (DREV, ISL) measuring a similar finned projectile differ by about 11 % in C_Nα and 0.4 cal in x_cp at M 3 (Dupuis & Berner, ISBS 2001, http://www.xrayct.com/documents/data/IBS19/EB11_495.pdf, Fig. 5); treat the high-supersonic points with that spread in mind, on top of the digitisation error.

The reference area is the body cross-section π d²/4 and the reference length d. Pitching moment is about the CG, so x_cp = x_cg − C_mα / C_Nα (calibers from the nose tip; C_mα < 0 is stable).
