The bowshock Guide

Performance

GPU or CPU

The GPU solver is written in plain Luma array ops and runs the same scheme as the f64 CPU solver (f32 or double-float arithmetic on the GPU). Measured per iteration on an RTX 3090 against the f64 CPU solver on 32 rayon threads (crates/bowshock-cfd/README.md, “Speed”; microseconds per iteration, 15 deg cone at Mach 2, 2000 iterations from the free stream, best of 3):

Viscous implicit step on the G3 body (crates/bowshock-cfd/README.md, “Viscous terms and the SST model”; SST, Mach 0.5, flat base and wake, microseconds per iteration, replay):

levelgridCPU f64GPU f32GPU double-float
0156x1226724319447

That is 21 to 26 times faster per iteration than the CPU in f32 (the CPU column moves by a factor of 2 between runs on a shared machine). Over the Fox TM X-2439 sweep (16 inviscid runs, level 1) the whole sweep took 1555.7 s on the CPU and 60.13 s on the GPU, 25.9 times faster, and 12.2 to 29.2 times per run (crates/bowshock-cfd/README.md, “Timing”). Double-float costs 1.2 to 1.4 times the f32 time in the viscous step.

The first call of a sweep also compiles and traces the GPU kernels: about 1 s for the default inviscid staging on the cone case, and longer for viscous cases (crates/bowshock-cfd/README.md, “Speed”, “First call”).

Resolution

Each Resolution level roughly doubles the grid along the body (156x122 at level 0, 312x122 at level 1 on the G3 body), so the per-iteration cost grows by 1.5 times (GPU f32) to 1.9 times (CPU) per level on that body, and finer grids also need more iterations to converge. Use Coarse for exploring, Medium for results and Fine for confirmation; see Analysis::estimate_uncertainty for a grid-sensitivity number.

The sweep is sequential over Mach numbers (cold starts by default, see warm start): cost is proportional to the number of points.

Total 177.9 s for 23 points (7.7 s per point on average). Points that stop by CdStationary take about 4000 iterations (4-8 s); points whose drag settles into a limit cycle (subsonic, and here M 2.4-2.5) stop by MeanStationary after at least 14000 iterations (14-19 s). Before the mean-stationary rule the subsonic points ran to the 160000-iteration cap: 142-154 s each.

Analysis::drag_tolerance(rel) (viscous only, off by default) replaces the default drag rule with a relative one: a point stops once its total drag varies by less than rel over the last 500 iterations, after the residual has dropped 2 orders. On the example rocket of the app (coarse grid, 10 points) 0.002 ended within 0.4 % of a 12000-iteration solution with about 2.8 times fewer iterations (29000 against 82000 in all); a drag that oscillates by about 1 % stops within 0.5 % of the mean of the cycle.
